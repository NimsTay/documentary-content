TITLE: THE COUNT WHO CONQUERED THE SKY
The Story of Ferdinand von Zeppelin

[SCENE 1 - COLD OPEN]

Today, flying feels ordinary.

But two hundred years ago, the idea of a giant ship floating through the air sounded like madness.

One man refused to believe it was impossible.

His name was Ferdinand von Zeppelin.

[SCENE 2 - THE SEED OF AN IDEA]

Ferdinand was born in 1838 in Konstanz, Germany, on the shores of Lake Constance. He grew up in a noble family and became an army officer.

In 1863, he traveled to America to observe the Civil War. And in Minnesota, he climbed into a hot air balloon.

For the first time, he saw the world from above.

But he also saw the problem. A balloon could rise... but it could not be steered. It went wherever the wind pushed it.

From that day on, one question stayed in his mind.

What if we could build a flying machine that we could actually control?

[SCENE 3 - THE BIG DESIGN]

In 1890, at the age of fifty-two, Ferdinand was forced to retire from the army.

Most people would have slowed down. Ferdinand did the opposite.

He designed a new kind of airship. Not a soft balloon, but a rigid one. A long, lightweight metal frame, covered in fabric, holding many separate cells of gas inside.

The frame kept its shape. The engines drove it forward. And a crew could steer it.

He patented the design in 1895. And then he began to build.

[SCENE 4 - THE WORLD LAUGHS]

Nobody believed in him.

Experts said it was impossible. Newspapers mocked him. Investors turned him away.

So Ferdinand used his own money, and started a company. He built a floating workshop on Lake Constance, and gathered a team of engineers.

[SCENE 5 - LZ 1]

On the second of July, 1900, his first airship, LZ 1, rose from the lake.

It was over one hundred and twenty meters long. Longer than a football field.

The engines roared. The giant lifted into the air.

It flew for only eighteen minutes, before a failure forced it down.

But it had flown. A rigid airship had taken off under power, and under control.

[SCENE 6 - FAILURE AFTER FAILURE]

But success did not come easy.

Money ran out. Later airships were damaged or destroyed. Each crash was another reason for the world to laugh.

Ferdinand was now in his late sixties. He kept building anyway.

[SCENE 7 - THE FIRE AT ECHTERDINGEN]

In 1908, his airship LZ 4 completed a flight lasting many hours across Germany and Switzerland. Crowds filled the streets to cheer.

Then, while it rested on the ground at Echterdingen, a storm tore it loose, and it burst into flames.

Years of work, gone in seconds.

It looked like the end.

[SCENE 8 - A NATION STEPS IN]

But the public had been watching.

Across Germany, ordinary people sent donations. Schoolchildren gave their pocket money. Workers gave a day's pay.

In just a few weeks, they raised millions of marks.

The people who once laughed at him... were now funding his dream.

[SCENE 9 - THE FIRST AIRLINE]

With that money, Ferdinand built again.

In 1909, his company launched the world's first airline, carrying paying passengers through the sky.

Before the First World War, tens of thousands of people flew on his airships, without a single serious injury.

The name zeppelin became the word for the airship itself.

[SCENE 10 - THE LEGACY]

Ferdinand von Zeppelin died in 1917, at the age of seventy-eight.

He never saw his airships cross oceans, or circle the entire globe.

But he proved something powerful. He started his greatest work when most people retire. He failed in front of the whole world. And each time, he stood back up.

Every impossible thing is only impossible... until someone refuses to accept it.

[END]
