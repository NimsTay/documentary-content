Scene #:
- Script: script explaining element
- visual prompt: what the image should depict based off the script. describe explicitly with realistic depictions instructions of what details should be like based on what is being described with respect to timeline, eras, people and subjects, etc
- direction: director notes for how the motion should work

Last element should contain a lesson like: "So what can we learn from Ferdinand von Zeppelin? He was not the youngest inventor. Not the richest. Not the most popular. He was simply the most stubborn."

* Script: Imagine standing on solid ground... and being told that a giant metal ship could float above your head. Today, that sounds ordinary. Planes cross the oceans every single day. But in the nineteenth century? It sounded like madness. And one man refused to believe it was impossible. His name was Ferdinand von Zeppelin.
* Image: a vintage sepia photograph style, 1890s Germany. In the foreground, a crowd of townspeople in period clothing (men in bowler hats and dark wool coats, women in long dresses and bonnets, children in sailor collars) all with heads tilted back, mouths open in wonder, shielding their eyes with their hands. Above them, filling the sky, a vast silver rigid cigar-shaped airship with a lightweight metal frame, rounded nose, tail fins and small engine gondolas underneath, glowing against dramatic towering clouds. Cobblestone street and old half-timbered houses at the bottom edge. Soft hazy light, film grain, slightly faded edges. Make sure the airship looks like an early rigid Zeppelin, not a modern blimp. 
* Motion: start with a close shot of the upturned faces, then slowly zoom out and tilt upward to reveal the full airship. Pan gently from bottom to top. Slow, awe-filled motion with a soft fade in from black.
